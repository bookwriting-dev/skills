# Testing the shape of an outline

Use the synopsis’s chosen form and the intended reader experience. Read consecutive chapter directives together and test a suspected weakness against what the reader would lose. Use these questions only where they fit the book.

## What keeps the middle alive?

Trace a choice into its consequence and the next choice. A sister files a petition to seal an archive to protect a living witness. The petition forces the protagonist to identify the contested letter in public. That act exposes the witness whom both sisters want to protect. Each chapter spends a choice made in the previous one. Three interviews that yield interchangeable leads can often move, merge, or go.

A supporting character’s objective should let you predict what they might do if the lead never arrived. Look for the point where that action collides with the main story. Test the collision by removing it and finding the later option that becomes available again.

## What changes if the goal stays the same?

A rescue can hold its initial goal throughout. The ferryman who once meant to bring everyone over together must choose whom the last crossing can carry. The goal persists while means, loyalties and cost change. A change of allegiance, revelation or reversal belongs where the story has earned it. Examine repeated chapter purposes for new cost, intimacy or understanding before recommending a cut.

## Which turns belong to this form?

A whodunit can turn on the interpretation of an old fact. In a five-act tragedy the recognition may come before a long fall. An epistolary braid may turn when the same signature appears on two incompatible accounts. A calm scene can finish a promise or let the reader see an old event differently. Evaluate the evidence that earns the intended turn. Keep chronological order, beat placement, goal changes and types of victory open to the book.

## What does the ending owe?

Name the question a reader can follow, the choice or experience that pays it, and any theme the ending leaves in tension. If the final image resolves the promised farewell, a fresh threat may damage the ending. If an announced confrontation is forgotten, identify the earlier promise and the missing consequence.
