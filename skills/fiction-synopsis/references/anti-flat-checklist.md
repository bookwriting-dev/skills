# Testing the premise before outlining

- **Whose situation is unstable?** Name what someone wants and what makes it harder here. A steady desire can deepen as the price changes. A false belief or changing goal can drive a transformation when it belongs to this protagonist.
- **What can continue for the requested length?** Show a first solution, the consequence it creates, and an independent action by a person or institution with a different stake. For a contemplative or episodic book, show what accrues each time the situation is revisited.
- **What matters personally?** Identify a relationship, home, belief, freedom or material need that the reader can recognize before it comes under pressure. Weather or an institution can supply meaningful pressure without inventing a villain.
- **What does the form offer the reader?** Choose the arrangement that earns the promised effect. A challenge in a rescue, a juxtaposition in a braid, and a returning image in a family chronicle may perform the same structural work.
- **What can the ending deliver?** State the concrete dramatic question and the outcome or experience it promises. Show how the events test a thematic question that admits more than one defensible reading.
