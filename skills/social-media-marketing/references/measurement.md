# Measurement and Retrospectives

## Principle

Track a few numbers that point to a decision. Vanity metrics (raw follower count, likes) matter less than saves, shares, clicks, signups, and sales.

## What to track

| Platform | Signals that matter | Weak signals |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Average watch time and completion rate, saves, shares, profile visits, link clicks | Likes alone, total views on a one-off viral video |
| Instagram | Saves, shares, Reel watch time, profile visits, link taps, Story replies | Likes alone |
| Facebook | Group engagement, event responses, link clicks, comments, shares | Page likes |
| All | Email signups, retailer clicks, pre-orders, sales and page reads, new reviews | Follower count alone |

## Linking social to sales

- Use one trackable link per platform (for example a link hub or tagged URLs) so clicks can be traced to a source.
- Record the launch-week sales and review count against the dates of each post.
- Treat sales data as indicative, not exact. Retail dashboards don't attribute sales to a post.

## Cadence

- **Weekly:** a five-minute check. Which post did best? Which did worst? Note the hook and format.
- **Every 4 weeks:** a retrospective that feeds the next calendar.

## Reading the numbers

- High views, low saves and clicks: the hook works, but the offer or call to action doesn't.
- High saves, low views: the content is good but the hook or timing limits reach. Re-cut the opening.
- High clicks, low sales: the landing page, price, or cover is the problem, not the social content.
- One viral post with no follow-through: capture the audience (email, series tie-in) rather than chasing a repeat.
- Sample sizes of a few posts are noisy. Look for a pattern across several before changing the strategy.
