# Opening-scene trial

Use the premise, viewpoint, and difficult choice from the request or the selected book's intake brief. Infer small provisional details when the premise supports them. Ask a single focused question when a missing premise or choice would change what the scene is about.

Write one short scene in prose. Start with an action that puts the choice under pressure. Give the viewpoint character a stake on both sides and end on the consequence of choosing. Use the specified voice or a provisional close viewpoint. Treat new names and details as trial material until promoted.

Save the first sample as `_Intake/Book-N/opening-scene.md` and return its path and the decision the scene put on the page. For another trial use a numbered sibling. Edit an existing sample when asked to revise it. The sample is the only project output of trial mode.

When the writer asks to promote a named trial, establish the destination and reconcile new details with existing canon. Register the chapter and use the normal scene planning, drafting, and chapter review workflow. Preserve the lines or beats the writer asked to retain.

## Worked example

Request — “Try my opening scene. Inez tends the ferry ramp. She has one berth left and has promised it to her brother. A stranger arrives with a feverish child.”

Excerpt — “Her brother's bag held the last place in line. Inez pulled it out from between the railings and held it against her ribs. 'Set her here,' she told the man. From the far end of the pier her brother whistled, twice, so she would know he'd made it.”

This scene ends with Inez giving away the berth while her brother can still see her. “Make this Chapter 1 and keep the two whistles” promotes that choice and detail into the normal chapter workflow.
